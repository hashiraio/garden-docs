---
title: "Slippage"
description: "Understand what the minimum on a quote means, and how slippage sets it"
---

Every quote from the [Aggregator API](/developers/api/aggregator) carries two amounts for what your user receives: `buy_amount`, the amount expected at the time of quoting, and `min_buy_amount`, the least the provider expects to deliver. The gap between them is your slippage tolerance.

## What the minimum means

`min_buy_amount` is an estimate, not a guarantee. Treat it as the expected worst case, not a promise you can hold a provider to.

It reaches you two ways, and a quote does not distinguish between them:

| | How the minimum is set |
|---|---|
| **Published** | The provider committed to a minimum and returned it. |
| **Derived** | The provider published no minimum, so it is calculated from `buy_amount` less your slippage. |

A derived minimum is computed from the provider's own quote, so it reflects your tolerance rather than any commitment.

<Warning>
  Do not present `min_buy_amount` to a user as an amount they are guaranteed to receive.
</Warning>

## Choosing a slippage value

`slippage_bps` is in basis points, where 100 is 1%. The default is 100. A higher value widens the gap between `buy_amount` and `min_buy_amount`, and a lower one narrows it.

On `exact_out`, slippage does not set `min_buy_amount`. It is `buy_amount`, or a minimum the provider published.

Some providers limit how much slippage they accept. A provider that cannot work with your value returns no quote and appears in `provider_errors` instead.
